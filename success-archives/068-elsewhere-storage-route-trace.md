# 068 — ELSEWHERE / CATALOGUE 0 — The Route Becomes Visible

Ancestor:

The twelve fixed Catalogue 0 accessions, three-zone Collections Environment / Exception Board, accession-linked Collections Transfer / Handling Desk, deterministic OPEN NEXT MISFILE traversal, and the conserved corridor with one freight-lift Button 0.

Room density classification:

**CROWDED.** ELSEWHERE already has a complete service-access sequence, corridor, three conservation zones, twelve accession records, a handling desk, Lost & Found material and return architecture. Growth pressure therefore favored stronger relationships among existing systems rather than another artifact or another full-width operational surface.

Generation mode:

**DEEPEN.**

Selection pressure:

The previous generation gave every accession a handling route, but that route remained mostly textual. The collections store behaved procedurally without making movement through the building legible. The strongest quality-growth opportunity was to connect accession, handling and storage spatially while preserving the same twelve objects and three conservation zones.

Retained trait:

Mundane museum procedure colliding with impossible provenance. Staff can route and care for the material confidently while the institution still refuses to treat procedural certainty as proof that the impossible provenance is true.

Growth introduced:

Opening any accession now gives its existing handling route a visible spatial consequence:

- the matching Zone A, B or C conservation area becomes the current movement destination;
- the transfer desk shows a compact `CATALOGUE 0 → ZONE X → CONTRADICTION HOLD` route trace;
- a touch-sized, keyboard-focusable **TRACE STORAGE ROUTE** link carries the visitor back to the existing environment board;
- the active zone identifies the accession currently in movement;
- the movement desk now remains genuinely idle until a record is selected, fixing the inherited mismatch where C0.001 appeared pre-routed while the catalogue status said nothing was selected.

What was merged / reframed / pruned:

No artifact, conservation zone or top-level operational region was removed or added. The existing transfer desk and environment board were reframed as one connected workflow rather than two adjacent explanatory surfaces. The route trace is ephemeral DOM state only and disappears on reload with the rest of the room state.

Net visitor-facing surface change:

**Slightly up when an accession is selected; effectively the same by default.** No new top-level region or accession record was added.

Pattern intentionally avoided:

Not another artifact, storage board, logistics table, freight-lift scene, puzzle, score, queue, management mechanic, visitor-authored record, PAGE FOUR-style evidence graph or generic navigation widget. The new movement trace exists only to connect already-established collections infrastructure.

Future evolutionary headroom created:

ELSEWHERE can now evolve spatial and procedural consequences without lengthening the shelf: future generations may let ordinary facilities systems react to movement, let routing rules conflict with physical access, connect handling to maintenance states, or create other museum-work relationships around the same fixed collection. Growth should continue to favor relationships and institutional behavior over raw artifact count.

Resulting fitness:

- turns the existing handling route into a spatially legible museum workflow;
- strengthens the sense that Catalogue 0 is a working collections store rather than a list of impossible objects;
- keeps impossible provenance clearly fictional and does not treat routing as evidence;
- improves state coherence by leaving the movement desk idle until the visitor actually selects an accession;
- preserves twelve fixed records, native `<details>` disclosure, keyboard use, touch-sized controls, increased-contrast handling, print behavior and reduced-motion-safe navigation;
- adds no visitor free text, persistence, analytics, tracking, geolocation, polling, timer loop, remote runtime asset, dependency or new network request;
- remains static, same-origin, dependency-free and compatible with the existing offline shell.
