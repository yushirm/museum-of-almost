### Mutation thesis

The Museum of Almost is now a browser-only law of nature: repairs increase coherence only when they remain visibly wrong. It is no longer an organism of equivalent knots, numeric tension, or a binary rule. The exiled fixation is the equivalent-node tension system that made every idea another peer target. The visitor is an obstacle and repeatedly repairs the timeline incorrectly. The topology is a touchable timeline whose seasons alter how error behaves. The governing contradiction is that the system is more coherent when partially broken. Three former mechanics survive only as one seasonal process: delayed consequences, inactivity-driven change, and one remembered event. Knot selection, tension values, bind/loosen language, the central field diagram, and anticipatory targeting are removed. Misregistered print and botanical growth stages organise density, timing, and feedback. This cannot be another layer on the previous system because its mechanic depends on replacing peer-node interaction with chronology, self-correction, and productive error. Why does this require the website itself to change? Because the old system could only express relationships between nodes, while this one exists through time.

### Preservation budget

Three mechanics survive, but none remains independently accessible:

1. Delayed consequences become later corrections on a different point in the timeline.
2. Inactivity becomes automatic self-repair that pushes the law toward excessive exactness.
3. Persistent memory becomes exactly one remembered repair, which returns one term late on the next visit.

All three alter the same seasonal misregistration field and therefore cannot be separated into features.
