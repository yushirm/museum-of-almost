## Mutation thesis

The Museum of Almost is now an organism made from unfinished ideas, held together by one unstable rule. It is no longer a building, a catalogue, or a set of destinations. The exiled fixation is the conversion of every idea into another named place. The visitor is the custodian of the rule and repeatedly contradicts it by binding what must remain separate or loosening what must remain joined. The topology is one continuous woven surface. Repetition produces difference: the same contradiction changes pressure, resistance, and delayed consequences each time. Two mechanics survive only in fusion: procedural fragments become living knots, while conservation movement becomes tension shared across the weave. Feature launchers, dialogs, collections, unlocks, rooms, photographic evidence, and independent modules are removed. Relationships, delayed causality, inactivity, and one retained accident now organise the experience. This cannot sit inside the former site because its meaning depends on eliminating independent destinations and making every action alter the same organism. Why does this require the website itself to change? Because the new work exists only when the old architecture stops containing it.

## Implementation outcome

The site is a single responsive field of six procedurally named knots joined by stateful fibres. Contradicting a knot changes its own pressure, a neighbour, the visible weave, the current wording, and a delayed consequence. Repeating a contradiction alternates its effect. Inactivity changes the anticipated knot and can reverse the rule. One seeded fictional accident may persist between visits. No destination, catalogue, collection total, unlock, modal feature, or independent mechanic remains.
