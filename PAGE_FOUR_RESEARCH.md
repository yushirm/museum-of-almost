# Page Four Research Ledger

This is the source ledger for Page Four’s evidence-intake wing.

The public gallery remains a fictional archive. This document records the real primary sources used to write the evidence cards and the boundaries that keep those sources from being turned into unsupported claims.

## Research rule

A government, scientific, or archival record proves that the record exists and says what it says. It does not automatically validate every claim, interpretation, rumor, or mythology associated with the subject.

For Page Four:

- **DOCUMENTED** means the statement is supported by the cited primary source.
- **LIMIT** states what the cited source does not establish.
- **PAGE FOUR NOTE** is editorial interpretation or fictional connective tissue and is not additional evidence.
- “Unidentified” is treated as a classification state, not as evidence of an extraordinary cause.
- Resolved anomalies remain useful because they show how additional evidence can change a story.

No third-party source is loaded at runtime. All research cards are local text. External URLs appear only in this repository ledger.

## PB-701 — Project BLUE BOOK

Primary source:

- National Archives, **Project BLUE BOOK — Unidentified Flying Objects**: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos

Source facts used:

- Project BLUE BOOK records were transferred to the National Archives and declassified.
- The Air Force recorded 12,618 sightings from 1947 through 1969.
- 701 remained categorized as “Unidentified.”
- The Air Force stated that it found no evidence that the unidentified sightings represented extraterrestrial vehicles or technological principles beyond then-known science.

Page Four boundary:

The 701 unresolved files are evidence of unresolved classification in that historical record. They are not evidence that 701 extraterrestrial craft were observed.

## BF-1977 — FBI Bigfoot hair examination

Primary source:

- FBI Vault, **Bigfoot Part 01 (Final)**: https://vault.fbi.gov/bigfoot/Bigfoot%20Part%2001%20%28Final%29/view

Source facts used:

- FBI records document a request for laboratory examination of hair submitted in connection with a Bigfoot investigation.
- A February 1977 FBI letter states that the hairs were examined by transmitted and incident light microscopy, including morphological comparison with hairs of known origin.
- The FBI reported that the submitted hairs were of **deer-family origin**.

Page Four boundary:

This is evidence about the submitted sample. It is neither proof of Bigfoot nor a universal disproof of every cryptid report. The important research lesson is narrower: a physical sample can constrain a story.

## RV-XCI / RV-LIII — STARGATE / GRILL FLAME remote-viewing records

Primary sources:

- CIA Reading Room, **Summary Remote Viewing Session XCI**, document CIA-RDP96-00788R000900970001-7: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900970001-7
- CIA Reading Room, **Summary Remote Viewing Session LIII**, document CIA-RDP96-00788R000900390001-1: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900390001-1

Source facts used:

- Declassified records document U.S. government-sponsored remote-viewing experiments and training sessions.
- Session XCI reported that the viewer’s impressions appeared to correlate with the geographic area indicated by coordinates that were later found not to point to the intended target.
- Session LIII reported no target correlation in that training attempt.

Page Four boundary:

The existence of the program and its records is documented. Individual session reports are not treated as proof of paranormal perception. The two records are deliberately paired because the archive contains both apparently encouraging and clearly unsuccessful session assessments.

## NOAA-1997 — The Bloop

Primary sources:

- NOAA Pacific Marine Environmental Laboratory, **Icequakes (Bloop)**: https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/acoustics/sounds/bloop.html
- NOAA Ocean Service, **What is the Bloop?**: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/bloop.html

Source facts used:

- A powerful low-frequency underwater sound was recorded across Pacific hydrophones in 1997.
- NOAA later found the sound consistent with icequakes generated by large icebergs cracking and fracturing.
- Similar signals were recorded and used to track iceberg activity.

Page Four boundary:

The anomalous recording was real. The later cryogenic explanation means the evidence does not require a biological or paranormal source.

## 1I/2017 U1 — ʻOumuamua

Primary source:

- NASA, **Our Solar System’s First Known Interstellar Object Gets Unexpected Speed Boost**, Release 18-056: https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/our-solar-systems-first-known-interstellar-object-gets-unexpected-speed-boost/

Source facts used:

- ʻOumuamua was the first known interstellar object observed passing through the solar system.
- Hubble observations showed a small nongravitational acceleration compared with a gravity-only trajectory.
- NASA described comet-like outgassing as the likely explanation for the extra acceleration.
- Its exact shape remained difficult to determine from the available observations.

Page Four boundary:

An unusual trajectory and uncertain shape are legitimate scientific questions. They are not, by themselves, evidence that the object was artificial.

## UAP-IST-2023 — NASA UAP data-methodology work

Primary source:

- NASA Science, **Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Independent Study**: https://science.nasa.gov/uap/

Source facts used:

- NASA commissioned an independent team to examine how UAP could be studied scientifically using available and future data.
- NASA stated that the limited number of high-quality observations prevents firm scientific conclusions about the nature of UAP events.
- The study emphasized improved data collection, calibration, metadata, analysis, transparency, and scientific rigor.

Page Four boundary:

The methodological problem is itself evidence worth preserving: an observation can remain unidentified because the record is too weak to support a conclusion. “Unknown” is not a substitute for a cause.

## Cross-case lattice

The interactive lattice on Page Four is deliberately editorial. Its connections are not presented as a hidden mechanism joining the cases.

The fixed connections are:

1. **Unknown is a data state** — Project BLUE BOOK + NASA UAP methodology.
2. **Test the sample, not the story** — FBI Bigfoot hair + NOAA Bloop.
3. **Attention is not validation** — STARGATE records + Project BLUE BOOK.
4. **Anomaly can have a natural cause** — ʻOumuamua + NOAA Bloop.
5. **Method matters more than mystique** — NASA UAP methodology + STARGATE records.
6. **The recurring phenomenon is uncertainty** — an editorial synthesis across all six files.

The last connection is the core Page Four research thesis: the most persistent “pattern” is often the human rush to explain an observation before the evidence is strong enough to carry the explanation.
